01 / OVERVIEW

A clearer image,
one step at a time.

Real images rarely suffer from just one degradation. Removing the most dominant one can reveal what needs attention next.

DDPR combines dynamic degradation planning with region-adaptive editing. A planner reassesses the current image after every restoration step, while a single All-in-One restoration model balances stronger restoration in degraded regions with content preservation in relatively clean areas.

Abstract

Generative models have shown strong potential for All-in-One image restoration. However, existing methods either process compound degradations in a single pass or lack dynamic reassessment of the dominant degradation, often leading to suboptimal restoration orders and residual degradations. They also apply uniform restoration across the image, without distinguishing regions requiring content preservation from those requiring stronger restoration, which may cause content hallucination. To address these limitations, we propose Dominant Degradation Planning for image Restoration (DDPR), a unified framework with latent residual-guided adaptive editing. The Dominant Degradation Planner identifies the current dominant degradation and reassesses the image after each restoration step. The Degradation-Aware Latent Residual Assessor predicts a region-level degradation gate from VAE latent residual supervision, guiding the Degradation-Gated Dual-Branch LoRA to adaptively balance content-preservation and degradation-restoration branches. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance among the evaluated open-source methods across synthetic and real-world degradation benchmarks.

02 / METHOD

Plan globally.
Restore locally.

One planner selects the next restoration objective. One shared model carries it out, with spatial control over the edit.

01

Identify the dominant degradation

The Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct planner predicts the next degradation, restoration instruction, decision reason, and stop signal from the current image.

02

Estimate local restoration needs

DLRA learns from the latent residual between the current image and its single-step target. At inference, the image and degradation prompt produce a spatial gate.

03

Balance preservation and restoration

DGDB-LoRA uses the gate to fuse two low-rank branches inside Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, adapting the update at each image token.
